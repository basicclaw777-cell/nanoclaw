# Orc — Operational Balance

**Date:** 2026-10-22
**Question:** Is this monitoring instrument operationally sound, or is it telling me a story I can't act on?
**Method:** Cross-referenced voice-registry (16 voices, 4 layers), telemetry stats (168 slots, 156 anomalies), and prescription data (5 agent fingerprints, 4 target states) for structural inconsistencies.

---

**Finding:**

**1. Monitoring-to-work ratio.** The Cathedral already runs 6 monitoring systems mapped into this probe: physician (drift), agent-health (scores), stress-battery (compression/identity/contradiction), emergence harvester (vitality/goals/dialogue/temporal), belief tracker (surprise), and smell test (waste). The harmonic probe adds a 7th layer — and it only detected novel signal through ONE computed feature: beatEnergy. That feature accounts for 40% of all 156 anomalies. The other three anomaly features (creativePulse 25%, harmonicity 19%, spectralEntropy 15%) haven't yet proven they surface anything the source monitors don't already report. Seven monitoring systems for five agents is a 1.4:1 monitor-to-agent ratio. The probe justified its existence with the Oct 19 beat-energy cluster (z=4.72) — a relational pattern no single-agent monitor caught. But one novel detection in 168 slots is thin. The instrument earns its keep only if beat-energy analysis becomes a standing operational signal, not if the full 16-voice apparatus runs continuously.

**2. Layer amplitude imbalance.** Emergence averages 0.8 amplitude. Stress averages 0.167. That's a 4.8:1 ratio. Two of three stress voices (compression, identity) sit at amplitude 0 — effectively silent. Only contradiction registers at 0.5, and even that maps to a chamber score of 5/10. Meanwhile the system layer (heartbeat at 1.0, synapse at 0.596) runs louder than the agent layer (avg 0.6). This means the probe's loudest signals come from emergence metrics and system pulse — the meta-layer — while the stress indicators that would flag actual distress are nearly inaudible. The stress-battery last ran test #63 on Oct 21 and scored the system FRAGILE. That FRAGILE verdict produced near-zero amplitude in two of three stress voices. Either the mapping from chamber scores to amplitude is miscalibrated (score 0 = amplitude 0 is too aggressive), or FRAGILE doesn't mean what the probe thinks it means.

**3. Agent-emergence disconnect.** The five agents grade out at A, B, C, C, D. Average: C+. But emergence voices are blazing: vitality=1.0, goals=0.9, dialogue=0.9, temporal=0.8. Goals reports 100% agent accuracy across all tracked domains. The prescription data shows all five agents at totalGap=0 against the deepSync target — perfectly aligned. Something doesn't add up. Either the emergence metrics are measuring accumulated momentum (14,373 vault entries, 344 inter-agent exchanges) that masks current mediocrity, or the agent grades are too harsh. The orc voice is the clearest case: healthScore 8 (highest of all agents), amplitude 0.8 (loudest), but grade D (worst). That D is doing the most work while being graded the worst. The grading system penalizes something the health score doesn't — likely stewardship quality vs raw activity. The emergence layer is riding on system-level compounding while individual agents underperform their own standards.

**4. The 68% anomaly rate.** 114 of 168 slots flagged anomalous. Only 54 slots are "normal." When your anomaly detector fires on 2/3 of all data, the detector is miscalibrated — not the system. The healthyNormal fingerprint was computed from those 54 non-anomaly slots, which means the baseline represents the minority state. beatEnergy baseline (0.719) sits between the actual range extremes (0.654–0.780), so the thresholds are reasonable in absolute terms, but the z-score sensitivity is set too tight. The creativePulse spike on Oct 20 hit z=20.20 — twenty standard deviations. That's not an anomaly, that's a regime change the model can't represent. The baseline needs re-anchoring: either widen the z-score threshold from ~2 to ~3, or segment the timeline into operational regimes (quiet vs active build sprints) with separate baselines per regime.

**So what:**

1. **Strip the probe to its one proven signal.** Beat-energy anomaly detection is the keeper. Run it as a lightweight daemon against agent-health updates. The full 16-voice rendering is communication theater until cymatic visualization proves operational utility.
2. **Fix stress-layer mapping.** A FRAGILE system should not produce near-zero stress amplitude. Proposal: floor stress voices at 0.2 amplitude when the battery overall is FRAGILE, regardless of individual chamber scores.
3. **Investigate the grade-emergence split.** Pull the grading rubric for orc (D) and boxing (A). If orc's D comes from stewardship debt while its health and output are highest, the grade is measuring the right thing — but the probe should surface that tension explicitly, not bury it in amplitude.
4. **Recalibrate anomaly thresholds.** Move z-score threshold to 3.0, or implement regime-aware baselines. A 68% anomaly rate is noise, not signal.
5. **Cap monitoring expansion.** No new monitoring system until an existing one is retired or merged. The next instrument should replace, not stack.
