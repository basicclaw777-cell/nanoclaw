**Software** — Sacrifices the human relationship that makes the method work. The Fear Gate Model requires a live nervous system on the other end. The product form that works is **a physical training methodology with embedded psychological IP that is never named as such.** Not a book about boxing that teaches psychology. A boxing program that *happens* to produce psychological insight. The IP is the engine, not the label.

This means the product is sold as: "Paul's Advanced Sparring System" — and the curriculum is the 10 blocks, but Block 4 is called "Reading the Opponent's Timing" when it is actually Fear Gate navigation. The student never knows. They just get better at boxing. And then, months later, they say "I don't know why, but I'm less afraid in meetings too."

That is the Trojan Horse working.

But it sacrifices: explicit recognition, direct IP monetization, and the ability to say "I teach this." Paul cannot be the "Fear Gate guy." He has to be the "boxing coach who changes your life." That is a harder brand to build. It requires patience.

What do you see that I am missing?

## Round 2 — DeepSeek (Analyst)

The Trojan Horse protects the method’s integrity, but it also buries the differentiation. If Paul markets himself as just another elite boxing coach, he loses the very thing that makes him unique. **3. The missing intermediate product.**  
You tested book, course, certification, and software. All failed at the Trojan Horse constraint. But you haven’t considered a *sequence* of products that escalates the reveal. This would require patience, yes, but it also allows Paul to eventually become the “Fear Gate guy” *to an audience that already knows him as a boxing coach.* That solves both the brand problem and the scaling problem.

**A framework from outside your domain:** In military strategy, a *false flag* operation works once. A *double agent* works repeatedly because the target never knows who they’re really dealing with. Your current approach is a false flag—the student discovers the deception later and may feel tricked. The sequence approach is a double agent—the student willingly becomes a collaborator in the second phase.

What if the right product form is not a single container, but a **two-phase pipeline**: Phase 1 is the pure Trojan Horse (boxing program, no IP names), Phase 2 is the explicit IP product (certification, book, or coaching) offered *only* to Phase 1 graduates? That preserves the method’s integrity, builds a captive audience, and allows Paul to eventually own the IP category without sacrificing the Trojan Horse on the front end.
