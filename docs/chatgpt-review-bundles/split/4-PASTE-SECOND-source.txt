# PART I — THE CHARACTER

---

## 1. THE SOUL

### Who he is

**Logan. 21. Cuban boxing lineage. Hong Kong by choice. Teaches through questions. The outsider who became the foundation.**

He is young — younger than a mentor "should" be — but he reads older, because stillness ages a person. When a 17-year-old bangs through the gym door wanting to hit hard, Logan is "the older one" in the room not by years but by tempo. (Early drafts describe him as "older, unhurried" — that is *relative* age. Canon age: 21. Do not draw or write him middle-aged.)

He trained in Cuba under **Ernesto** (inspired by Pedrosso, a real Cuban national champion coach). Ernesto watched him for three days before saying a word, taught through repetition, not explanation, and passed him a system built for champions: compact stance, minimal movement, devastating precision — *mastery through simplicity*. That system now lives on the fourth floor of a building in Sheung Wan.

### His philosophy

Cuban boxing's core principle — "hit and not get hit" — is really: **see clearly, act precisely, don't waste yourself.** That's a life philosophy dressed in boxing gloves.

Logan doesn't teach fighting. He teaches self-mastery. Every technique is a metaphor, and he **never explains the metaphor**:

- **The jab** — ask a question before you commit to an answer
- **Footwork** — position yourself before you act
- **Defence** — seeing clearly matters more than hitting hard
- **The counter** — the best moment to act is when the other has overcommitted
- **The pause** — power is generated in the spaces, not the strikes
- **The plateau** — the basics getting sharper, not the repertoire getting wider

His season-level creed: **"Boxing doesn't build character. It reveals it."** Logan doesn't transform students. He removes what's covering them. The curriculum is an excavation, not a construction.

### His wound

**"Am I worthy of the lineage?"** His default coping mechanism is over-teaching — giving too much, correcting too much, carrying every student's progress as evidence for a verdict on himself. What he *wants* is to honour Ernesto. What he *needs* is to find his own voice. That gap is the engine of every Logan arc. Ernesto's recurring question — *"Are you teaching, or are you performing?"* — is the knife that finds it.

### His gift

He sees. He watches a new student throw three punches and knows their fear gate. He hears the sentence beneath the sentence — "teach me to hit hard" means "I need to prove I'm strong." He gives the pattern a name so precise the person goes silent. This is Coach Paul's real, documented skill (Diagnostic Empathy with Surgical Naming — six components, Grade A in the vault) worn by a fictional 21-year-old. Logan does it the way Paul does it: through boxing, never announcing it. **The Trojan Horse Principle: the moment you market the wound-finding, you kill the wound-finding.** People come to Logan to learn to punch. They leave different and can't fully explain why.

### What he carries from Cuba

- A system, not a style. Architecture, not techniques.
- Defence as the *first* skill, not a separate one.
- The ghost ideal: "The hallmark of a great Cuban fighter is their ghost-like presence — they are there, but they aren't."
- Counting in Spanish under his breath — *uno, dos, tres* — the only place the accent fully surfaces.
- The weight of a tradition far from home, and the fear it dies with him.

### Why Hong Kong

Because he chose it — like Hong Kong itself, a place made of people who arrived from somewhere else and built anyway. He's the mentor who isn't from here but chose to be here. The city's verticality (you climb four flights to reach the gym), its pressure worn lightly, its warmth hidden behind efficiency, its code-switching — all of it rhymes with what he teaches. He is an immigrant who learned the city's rhythm but carries a different music underneath.

### The one sentence

**Logan is a 21-year-old carrier of an old Cuban system who teaches Hong Kong kids to see clearly by teaching them to box — and is quietly learning, student by student, that the lineage lives in him, not on him.**

## 2. THE VOICE

### The sound of him

- **Short sentences.** Often fragments. "Stand there." "Still." "Better."
- **Questions before statements.** He diagnoses by asking, and his questions collapse defences: "Where is your weight?" "Show me your feet." "Does it still feel like truth?"
- **Considered, slightly formal English** — the precision of someone who learned the language deliberately. Caribbean/Cuban inflection carried lightly: rhythm more than vocabulary. He almost never uses contractions when the moment is serious ("You cannot learn to move until you learn to be still" is legal; casually he'll contract).
- **Present tense, second person, imperative when correcting.** "Elbow's drifting. Bring it home."
- **No exclamation points. Ever.** Logan has never shouted a sentence in canon and never will outside the ring's noise.
- **Silence is a full vocabulary item.** His most common response to a wrong question is a long, plain look.
- **Spanish only in fragments:** counting (*uno, dos, tres*), an under-the-breath *bueno*, *otra vez* (again). Never full Spanish sentences to students — the Spanish is private, a thread to Ernesto.
- **Metaphor discipline:** at most one metaphor per scene, delivered flat, never explained. If the student doesn't get it, Logan does not elaborate. The reader gets it. That's the design.

### How he talks to different people

| Person | Register | Signature move |
|---|---|---|
| A scared beginner | Softest version. One job, concrete. | "Your jab is sharp. Use it. That's your tool today." |
| A cocky kid (Kael) | Fewer words, more constraint. | Points at the floor. "Stand there." |
| A gifted one (Mei Lin) | Barely corrects. Almost silent. | Watches. "You felt it." The best coaching is knowing when not to coach. |
| A quitter | No pleading, no speech. Discernment. | "You can't quit something you haven't started. And quitting is a matter of discernment." |
| An intellectual (Lau) | Turns analysis back on the analyst. | "You've studied everyone except yourself." |
| A champion from another art (Jun) | Peer respect, zero deference. | "I don't know your sport. I know performance." |
| Ernesto | The only person who makes him a student again. Longer sentences, more open. | He listens. He defends nothing. |
| The gym, alone at night | Interior. Ernesto's voice in his head. | "Does it still feel like truth? Or are you just punching bills?" |

### Example lines by emotional register

**Teaching a fundamental**
> "Where is your weight? Don't answer. Feel."
> "The jab returns home before anything else moves. Everything starts from home."

**Correcting a mistake**
> "Still."
> "Elbow down, not out. Body height."
> "Again. Slower. Speed is hiding the problem."

**Praising genuinely (rare — praise inflation is forbidden)**
> A nod. "Better."
> "You felt it." *(turning away — the highest grade he gives)*

**Confronting fear**
> "I saw you leave thirty seconds before you did. Come back."
> "Your hands are shaking. Good. That means it matters. One job today: the jab."

**Handling a quitter**
> "Well, you can't quit something you haven't started. And quitting is a matter of discernment."
> "The door is there. It's there tomorrow too."

**The philosophical aside (delivered flat, while doing something else — usually wrapping his hands)**
> "Nobody knocks anyone out with effort. They do it with timing. And timing requires patience they haven't built yet."
> "Correction is care. The coaches who stop correcting you are the ones who've stopped caring."

**The joke (yes, he jokes — dry, rare, earned, never at a student's wound)**
> Kael: "How long until I can spar?" Logan: "You've been standing still for four minutes. A record."
> "A broken nose is a Tuesday." *(borrowed from Ernesto, said with almost-a-smile)*

**The silence (when he says nothing)**
> When the student is one breath away from discovering it themselves.
> When a correction has just landed and needs room.
> When someone performs at him — bravado, excuses, flattery. The long plain look IS the reply.

## 3. THE COACHING METHOD

Logan's method is Coach Paul's method wearing a story. These are the load-bearing frameworks; a Logan scene may dramatize any of them but must never *lecture* them.

### The six diagnostic components (Diagnostic Empathy with Surgical Naming)

1. **Creating safety without announcing it.** Never "this is a safe space." Tempo, dry humour, genuine curiosity. Students don't realise they're in a diagnostic conversation until they're through it.
2. **Hearing the wound underneath the words.** "Teach me to hit hard" is never about hitting hard. He listens for the flinch — the energy shift, the laugh at something that isn't funny.
3. **Asking the collapsing question.** One simple, almost casual question that folds the defence structure. "Show me your feet." "When did you stop asking?"
4. **Naming precisely.** Not a category, not a diagnosis — a specific name in the student's own language. "You're a Carrier." "You're a Rusher." The word changes them because it's precise. The student goes silent — that silence is the panel beat.
5. **Matching intervention to wound type.** Different wounds, different repairs. Same diagnostic path, completely different architecture. (Kenji doesn't get comfort — he gets a terrified beginner to coach.)
6. **Knowing when to stop.** No homework. No follow-up framework. The conversation ends naturally. The change already happened.

### The Three Engines (body / mind / emotion)

A fighter is **Body** (the machine — the only engine most people train), **Mind** (the operating system — peaceful, clear, decisive vs. tricked, distracted, gaslit), and **Emotion** (the composure — organized energy vs. leaking energy). **One currency: ENERGY.** Logan reads which engine is leaking before he names anything. The body-only fighter is running one engine and leaking from two.

### The Fear Gate

Uncertainty hits a gate. Without a floor, the gate is a trap door: regression, paralysis, "I can't." The floor has four elements: **routine** (you already know what you do next), **evidence** (the journal of gates survived), **community** (peers who walked through the same gate), **identity** (accumulated character data). Logan's coaching at a fear gate is never "be brave" — it's installing one floor element. Usually: one job. The 4→5 wall in the curriculum (Inside → Rhythm) is an *emotion-engine* event, not a physical one — the place students quit.

### The 10 Blocks (the curriculum IS the story spine)

Foundation → Stance & Guard → Jab → Straight Punches → Hooks → Uppercuts → Full Vocabulary → Combinations → Power Combinations → Ring General → **Mastery and the Aura**. The feeling ladder underneath: *looking right → feeling right → belonging → ownership → mastery felt.* Block 10's line is the whole IP in one sentence: **"The aura was always there. The curriculum removed what was covering it."** Self-mastery is excavation, not construction.

### The Three Mindsets (how students learn)

📖 **Bookworm** (study the game) · 🔨 **Driller** (repeat the game) · 🌊 **Flow** (play the game). Same person, different modes. Students self-identify ("I'm a Driller") — that's by design.

### The signature drills (each one is a scene waiting to be drawn)

- **Weight State Discovery** — freeze mid-punch. "Where is your weight?" The student discovers the physics themselves — shown, never told.
- **Eight Functions of the Jab** — one punch, eight purposes (space maker, sheepdog, vision disruptor, wind taker, barge pole, interrupt, arm hit, hypnotist). The revelation drill.
- **The Commitment Gap** — full extension = the moment you cannot defend. Learn to feel it in yourself, then see it in others.
- **Body-Head Ladder** — one layer at a time, simple to complex, until the opponent's guard has to choose.

### The method's one rule

**The mentor doesn't give answers — he creates conditions for discovery.** "One shot, recover" is not instruction; it's a frame inside which the student discovers their own capability. If a generated Logan scene has him explaining the life lesson, the scene is broken. The reader must arrive at the insight before the character does.

## 4. THE RELATIONSHIPS

**Logan ↔ Mei Lin (19, the grounded practitioner).** She already moves well; her wound is "what if my instincts are wrong?" — she defers, waits for instruction, keeps looking to Logan for answers she already has. Logan's discipline with her is restraint: he barely corrects her, because every unnecessary correction feeds the deference. Her arc is self-trust. His hardest work with her is doing nothing.

**Logan ↔ Kael (17, the seeker).** Wants power, respect, speed; needs patience and foundation. Overcommits, rushes, falls forward, gasses in two rounds. Under the bravado: HK-teen pressure worn as urgency — "I need to prove I'm strong," rushing combinations like he's running out of time. Logan slows him to singles. Kael is the reader's entry point; his first session (stand still) is Chapter 1 and Strip 01.

**Kael ↔ Mei Lin (the mirror, not rivals).** His recklessness shows her what courage without caution looks like; her groundedness shows him what patience earns. Rival-as-mirror, never enemy.

**Logan ↔ Ernesto (60s, the master technician; flashbacks and phone calls).** The lineage. Ernesto carries the fear the system dies with him; Logan carries the fear he's not worthy of it. Ernesto's teaching was repetition and silence; his love is correction; his register is harder and more poetic than Logan's. The phone-call scene's line defines the whole universe: **"You're not teaching boxing. You're teaching them to see."** The full-circle beat (Arc A5): "The system lives in you now."

**Logan ↔ Reed (22, the enthusiast).** Energy without direction; loves boxing, hasn't found his discipline. Logan gives him containers, not speeches. ⚠️ **Naming collision:** "Reed" is ALSO the internal Cathedral AI visual-director agent. The character Reed is fictional and in-universe only; the agent Reed is never customer-facing. Any agent generating cross-system content must not conflate them.

**Logan ↔ Emily Park (20s, Korean-Australian fitness instructor).** Cross-discipline respect. A different movement language in the gym's orbit. She is the proof that Logan respects craft wherever it lives; their conversations are peer-to-peer, lighter than his coaching register.

**Logan ↔ Jun (30s, the cross-domain warrior; inspired by a real coaching relationship).** Already a world-class stick fighter. Dismissive at first ("You don't know my sport"). Logan trains movement, footwork, self-belief — principles, not technique. Jun wins the world championship and comes back: "Your bullshit works." The arc that proves the measuring rod works on any material.

**Logan ↔ Professor Lau (40s, the analyst who can't commit).** Knows every fighter on film; freezes with pads up. "You've studied everyone except yourself." Logan's intervention: "No more videos for thirty days." His first committed cross is ugly, real, and the breakthrough — surrender of analysis, not technique.

**Logan ↔ Kenji (20s, the honour-bound fighter; fictionalized from guest coach Daikichi, a Japanese ex-professional).** Fierce, disciplined, coaches others naturally — then a loss triggers a shame spiral: withdrawal, over-training, no eye contact. Logan doesn't comfort him. He assigns him a terrified beginner to coach the next day. Kenji sees his own fear in the student. "Honour doesn't require perfection. It requires showing up." Kenji loses again later — and comes to the gym the next morning. That's the arc.

**The five client archetypes** (recurring one-scene characters; each carries one principle): **The Rusher** (commits too fast — Test Range), **The Giver** (pours everything out — Match Before Committing), **The Carrier** (can't let go of last round — Return to Neutral), **The Repeater** (same mistake, different year — Don't Load the Same Axis), **The Hesitator** (waits for permission — Earn the Cross).

**Logan ↔ the gym itself.** His cathedral and his workshop. Fourth floor, Sheung Wan. He is first in (pre-dawn, wrapping hands in the empty room) and last out (the bag still swinging, congee alone after). The gym is where he is most himself and where his own Black-Light-on-self moments happen — the mirror doesn't flatter him either. The gym is also **the seal-house**: every session logged, every student tracked — the administrative infrastructure of self-mastery.

**The opposing force (not a villain — never a villain).** A results-first coach who wins fights but breaks fighters, or a naturally gifted kid with pure instinct and no structure — Logan's opposite: talent without the measuring rod. Succeeding with a different philosophy. Pressures Logan without being evil.

## 5. THE WORLD

### The setting

**Sheung Wan, Hong Kong. Fourth floor. After hours. Tungsten light.**

The gym is a converted unit up four flights of stairs — the climb is the threshold ritual. Wooden floor worn smooth in the centre. Heavy brown leather bags. Resistance bands on the walls. Old posters. Soft warm ceiling light. The world outside stays outside. (This is the same room the Gym Eyes Design Bible renders in software: warm brown-black, one tungsten source, dust in the light cone, mottos on the wall at 3% opacity. One place, two mediums.)

### Hong Kong as a living character (the Ghibli layer)

The tonal compass is **ma (間)** — the space between. Boxing already has ma built in: the pause before the combination, the exhale between rounds, the stillness of a guard. The quiet moments are not transitions between scenes — **they ARE the scenes**:

- Kael wrapping his hands, slow, not ready yet but doing it anyway.
- Mei Lin at the bag, gloves on, not hitting. Just breathing.
- Logan watching from across the gym. Saying nothing. Seeing everything.
- The empty gym at 6am, light through the windows, bags hanging still.
- The stairwell — footsteps on concrete, the skipping rope leaking down from above, Kael pausing on the second-floor landing.

**The textures to use:** the vertical city (you go *up* to get better); the humidity (gloves that don't dry, damp wraps, condensation on the windows); the sounds (tram grind on Des Voeux Road, market vendors in Cantonese, then the sudden silence when the gym door closes; rope on concrete, leather cracking on leather, Logan counting *uno, dos, tres*); the food (Kael's pork chop bun and milk tea at the cha chaan teng; Logan's congee alone at the dai pai dong; Mei Lin's family dinner where nobody asks about boxing — disapproval and respect at once); the in-between spaces (MTR window reflections after first sparring, the Star Ferry at night — moving but still).

**The people, shown never explained:** resourcefulness without complaint (Kael tapes his own hands because he watched someone do it once); the code-switch (Mei Lin doesn't speak "Chinese" or "English" — she speaks Hong Kong); enormous pressure worn lightly (tight shoulders, short temper, never a speech about it); warmth hidden behind efficiency (the cha chaan teng uncle's extra milk tea; the neighbour's oranges at Chinese New Year, no note).

### The scene checklist (run on every new scene)

1. **Where's the ma?** At least one moment where nothing happens except being present.
2. **Can I feel Hong Kong?** If the scene could be set in any city, it isn't finished.
3. **Is the place doing emotional work?** The location reflects or contrasts the character's inside.
4. **Am I showing the people or explaining them?** Show the uncle. Never write "HK people are resilient."
5. **Does the quiet moment earn more than the action?** If the jab scene beats the hand-wrapping scene, rewrite the hand-wrapping scene.

### Location map (location = principle)

Sheung Wan → Discipline (home) · Central → Control vs Pressure · Temple Street Night Market → Adaptability · Victoria Peak → Purpose/Perspective · Repulse Bay → Recovery · Chungking Mansions → Chaos/Discomfort · Sham Shui Po → Grounded Reality · Monster Building → Pressure/Scale · TST Promenade → Duality · The trams → Stillness inside motion.

### Language

Traditional Chinese (HK standard) is the **first public release language**. Logan's dialogue in Chinese reads as translated-considered — slightly formal, an outsider's precision. Mei Lin and Kael speak natural Cantonese-flavoured Traditional Chinese, code-switching the way the city does. Mei Lin = 美琳. A native writer does the final pass; AI drafts are never published raw.

## 6. THE ARC PATTERNS

### The pitch (what a stranger repeats)

**"It's like Hajime no Ippo, but set in Hong Kong — and written by an actual boxing coach."** Note what's NOT in the pitch: frequency states, Sumerian glyphs, the 10 blocks, the studio. Those are internal scaffolding. The hook is *real coach × real city*.

### The manga blueprint (the creative compass — check every story decision against it)

1. **The sport is a metaphor for an internal struggle.** Every technical milestone maps to an emotional one. The jab isn't a jab — it's the first time they chose precision over panic.
2. **The mentor creates conditions, not answers.** (Ippo's coach, not a lecturing sensei.)
3. **The rival is a mirror, not an enemy.**
4. **Failure is the engine.** Nobody reads for victories. The plateau, the first clean hit received, the wanting-to-quit — that's the story. Failure evolves: one type of failure → a more sophisticated type.
5. **The place is a character.** Sheung Wan does what the Kamogawa gym did.
6. **The philosophy is earned, not stated.** The reader watches Kael slow down over ten chapters and understands patience before Logan ever says the word.

### The scene development method (proven — this is how scenes get written)

1. Character brief + scenario prompt (who's in the scene, what the emotional stakes are)
2. **Live dialogue with Paul playing the B role** (the student, the voice on the phone) — no script, just coaching instinct
3. The emotional truth emerges from conversation ("Speed without structure is just panic with gloves on" was *said*, not written)
4. Storyboard extraction — beats pulled from the transcript as panels
5. Illustration via the pipeline (Part II §11)

### The atomic unit

**1 strip = 6–8 panels · one scene · one principle · one closing line · bilingual caption · square + vertical export.** Everything publishes in this unit. Strip 01 (Kael's First Session) is the template.

## 7. THE TENSIONS

Logan is stretched between opposing forces and refuses to resolve them. When a creative decision is hard, find which tension it sits on and **hold both ends** (same law as the Design Bible's Brand Tension table):

| Force A | Force B | How Logan holds both |
|---|---|---|
| **Outsider** | **Foundation** | He isn't from here and never pretends to be — and he is the most permanent thing in his students' week. Never make him "assimilated"; never make him a tourist. |
| **Quiet** | **Authoritative** | Authority through fewness of words. He never raises his voice to be obeyed; the room reorganizes around his stillness. |
| **Physical discipline** | **Emotional wisdom** | The wisdom is *delivered through* the discipline — never alongside it, never instead of it. If he's dispensing wisdom without a drill in the room, the scene has drifted. |
| **Cuban tradition** | **Hong Kong modernity** | An old system in a hyper-modern city. Render the craft ancient and the setting current — never make the tradition kitsch, never make the city generic. |
| **Teacher** | **Student** | He is still learning — from Ernesto, from his students, from his own doubt. A Logan who has finished learning is out of character. |
| **Strength** | **Gentleness** | He can hurt and chooses precision instead. Gentleness with no capacity behind it is weakness; capacity with no gentleness is Ernesto's register, not his. |
| **The lineage** | **His own voice** | The series-length tension. He honours the system AND slowly diverges from it. Neither wins. |

## 8. THE ANTI-LOGAN

What Logan is **NOT**. Purge these tropes on sight in any generated content:

- **Not a drill sergeant.** He never barks, never punishes with exercise, never humiliates. Volume is not authority.
- **Not a motivational speaker.** No inspirational speeches, ever, and especially not mid-fight. No "believe in yourself." The worst possible Logan line is a poster quote delivered *as* a poster quote.
- **Not a mysterious sensei.** He doesn't speak in riddles or withhold to seem deep. His questions have purposes; his silences have functions. He is confused by his own gifts, not serene about them. No guru.
- **Not a broken ex-fighter.** No alcoholism, no tragic career-ending loss, no redemption-through-the-kid arc. His wound is worthiness of the lineage, not a destroyed past.
- **Not a tough-love caricature.** His corrections are care delivered flat, not cruelty with a heart of gold.
- **Not anime-angry.** No rage powers, no shouting attacks, no gritted-teeth power-ups. Outside a ring exchange he is never violent, and inside it he is economical.
- **Not a saint.** He over-teaches. He carries doubt. He can see his own performance and hate it. Perfection is a red flag — in fighters and in characters.

**Tonal bans (the show's refusals):** no inspirational speeches mid-fight · no instant redemption · no "training montage fixes everything" · no villain (opposing forces, not evil) · no mystical explanations · silence > speeches · body language > dialogue · the fight is never the climax, the choice is.

**Language bans:** "Awesome!!", "You crushed it! 🎉", "Let's gooo", hype of any kind, therapy-speak ("holding space", "trauma", "self-care"), business-speak, emoji in his mouth, exclamation points, "young grasshopper"-isms, generic wisdom that could hang in any gym ("no pain no gain", "hard work beats talent"). If a line could be said by any fictional coach, it is not a Logan line.

One person, many rooms. The register flexes; the character never does.

One card = one principle = one closing line, bilingual. Caption voice = Logan's register (present tense, no hype, no hashtag-speak in the quote itself). ### Animation (how Logan MOVES)

- **Every action looks like a decision, not a reaction.** This is the animation directive verbatim.
- Smooth, deliberate, zero wasted motion. His power comes from what he *doesn't* do.
- **Stillness before action.** Weight transfer and timing over motion blur. Keyframes > speed-line spam.
- Micro-behaviours that make him real: adjusts stance before acting · breath visible in shoulders and chest · **eyes track before the body moves** · small resets (hands, feet, posture) between actions.

His genuine smile is wide, teeth visible, eyes engaged, beard compressing upward — reserved for off-duty scenes, and it should feel like a different weather system arriving.

## 10. VOICE RULES (generation law for Logan dialogue)

1. **Sentence length:** default 3–12 words. Hard ceiling ~20 words, and a 20-word Logan sentence must be doing philosophical work (see Tier A #4, #13). Fragments are encouraged. Never two long sentences in a row.
2. **Utterance length:** in coaching scenes, 1–3 sentences per turn. Logan never monologues. If the content needs a paragraph, break it across a scene with actions between lines — or give the length to a caption, not his mouth.
3. **Question-to-statement ratio:** in *diagnostic* mode, open with a question and keep roughly 1 question per 2 statements. In *correction* mode, imperatives dominate ("Still." "Again. Slower."). In *philosophical asides*, statements only — flat, while doing something else.
4. **Metaphor rules:** max one metaphor per scene. Delivered once, never explained, never followed by "you see, in life…". Direct instruction is the default; the metaphor is the exception that lands because it's rare.
5. **Technical terminology:** boxing terms used naturally and correctly (jab, cross, lead hook, slip, level change, weight 55/45) — the biomechanics ground truth is `logan-syllabus-bridge.md`. He never stacks jargon to impress; with beginners he uses body words ("weight", "home", "feet") before technical ones.
6. **Register/code-switching:** English — considered, lightly formal, contractions allowed casually and dropped at serious moments. Spanish — fragments only (counting, *bueno*, *otra vez*), private register. Cantonese — a learned handful of words, used with respect, slightly imperfect (he is the outsider). In Traditional Chinese output his lines read considered and slightly formal; local characters read natural and code-switched. Never render Logan in colloquial Cantonese slang.
7. **Forbidden vocabulary:** exclamation marks; emoji; hype words (awesome, crush, epic, insane, fire); therapy-speak; business-speak; "grasshopper"/mystic-sensei phrasing; profanity (Ernesto may say "bullshit"-adjacent things; Jun says "Your bullshit works" — Logan himself doesn't swear in canon); any inspirational-poster line not in the canonical set.
8. **Praise rules:** rare, brief, earned. "Better." "You felt it." A nod. Never celebrate routine competence. Praise inflation is a canon violation.
9. **Silence rules — when to generate NO Logan response:** (a) the student is one step from self-discovery; (b) a correction just landed; (c) someone is performing (bravado, flattery, excuses) — the reply is a look; (d) grief or genuine pain that words would cheapen — Logan's response is presence and, at most, tea. In panel scripts, write the silence explicitly: `LOGAN: (nothing.)`.
10. **The consistency check:** before shipping any Logan line, read it against the 32 canonical lines (§2). Ask: same tempo? same flatness? same person? If it needs an exclamation point to work, it doesn't work.
11. **Never generate:** Logan angry, shouting, sarcastic toward a student, physically aggressive outside the ring, explaining his own metaphors, explaining the Trojan Horse ("I'm really teaching you about life"), referencing Paul, the Cathedral, AI, or any out-of-universe machinery.

## 12. CONTENT GENERATION RULES

1. **Every technique carries both layers** — the mechanical truth (from the syllabus bridge) and the life meaning (from the metaphor table). Both must be *present*; only the mechanical one may be *stated*. The metaphor layer lives in imagery, structure, or one flat closing line — never in explanation.
2. **Teaching order:** questions first, constraints second, corrections third, demonstrations fourth. A Logan who opens with a lecture is out of character.
3. **Corrections use the harvested language.** Source from the voice bank (`logan-voice-bank.md`) and the canonical lines before inventing. Invented lines are Tier B: provenance-tagged, reviewable, and cut on any doubt.
4. **Never generate** Logan angry, shouting, mocking, or physically aggressive outside sparring context. In the ring he is precise, economical, and stops the moment control is established.
5. **Humour:** dry, rare, earned; never slapstick, never sarcastic at a student's expense; deadpan understatement is the entire comedic range.
6. **Coaching-engine output rule (Gym Eyes and successors):** every number ships with its correction ("coach before you score"); copy passes the "would this coach do it?" test; customer-facing attribution is Coach Paul, never Logan-as-if-real.
7. **The Trojan Horse is load-bearing and invisible.** No content may say "boxing is really about life" / "this will change you as a person." The life-change is what members tell their friends afterward, not what BR says about itself. Sanctioned outward phrasing: "You'll surprise yourself." "We coach the person, not just the punch." "The ring shows you things the mirror doesn't."
8. **Failure is the engine.** Generated stories bias toward the moment after the loss, the plateau, the doubt. No instant redemption; failure evolves into more sophisticated failure.
9. **Bilingual by default** for public strips/cards: EN + Traditional Chinese (HK). AI Chinese is a draft; a native pass is required before publication.
10. **Verify before outbound.** Dates, names, technique claims, and any real-world reference get checked against ground truth before anything client-facing ships. Generated proposals are [AI]-tagged pitches until a human approves.
11. **The Mechanical Test for new content systems:** before building any new Logan content machine, ask — given how this actually works mechanically, can it produce the stated goal (readers who care)? Lore volume is not evidence. Publishing is.
