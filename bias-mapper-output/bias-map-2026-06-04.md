### 1. Attention × Underweight
**Status:** EMPTY
**Response:** **Genuinely empty — this combination may not produce a distinct bias.**  
The logic of "underweighting" at the attention stage would mean failing to allocate sufficient attentional resources to a stimulus that is objectively important. However, the documented bias that comes closest is **Inattentional Blindness** (Mack & Rock, 1998), where a salient stimulus is missed entirely (a delete error, not underweight). Underweighting attention is better captured by **limited capacity models** (e.g., Kahneman, 1973) rather than a specific bias. The combination may be a structural gap because attention is typically binary (attended vs. unattended) in experimental paradigms, making graded underweighting a measurement artifact rather than a distinct cognitive bias.
