# Forensic Relay — ancient construction — Phase 1 Prompt

You are a forensic analyst with no prior knowledge of ancient construction. Your task is to derive the maximum possible leveling precision achievable with copper tools, wooden levels, and water-filled trenches. Do not import any assumptions about ancient Egyptian capabilities. Start from first principles: copper hardness, wood flexibility, water surface tension, and human visual acuity. Calculate the best possible tolerance in mm over a 230-meter distance. Then compare this to the measured tolerance of the Great Pyramid's base: 15 mm over 230 m (1:15,000). If your derived precision is worse than 15 mm, explain step by step how the ancient builders could have exceeded it using only the tools described. If your derived precision matches or exceeds 15 mm, show your derivation in full detail. After completing your derivation, perform a self-audit: list every assumption you made and state whether each assumption is derived from observation or imported from external knowledge. If any assumption is imported, flag it explicitly. Output your response in the following format:

## Derivation
[Your step-by-step derivation]

## Comparison
[Comparison to 15 mm tolerance]

## Self-Audit
[List of assumptions, each marked as 'derived' or 'imported']

## Conclusion
[One sentence stating whether the precision is achievable with the described tools, and if not, what this implies about the orthodox model]
