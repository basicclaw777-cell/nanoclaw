## Meta-Extraction — The Three Questions

### What Am I Not Asking?

- **Why do amyloid-clearing drugs that fail in sporadic Alzheimer's show some efficacy in early-onset familial Alzheimer's?**
  _Why:_ This would test whether the amyloid hypothesis is valid for a subset but not the majority, forcing a refinement or stratification of the orthodoxy.
  _Extends:_ Crack #1 (failed drug trials) and Phase 1 (deriving the amyloid-cognition link)

- **What is the role of APOE4 genotype in the amyloid cascade, and why does it increase risk but not universally cause disease?**
  _Why:_ APOE4 is the strongest genetic risk factor, yet many carriers remain cognitively normal. This challenges the simple causal chain and suggests other factors (e.g., lipid metabolism, neuroinflammation) are critical.
  _Extends:_ Crack #2 (amyloid in normals) and Phase 2 (alternative model)

- **How do microglial activation and neuroinflammation interact with amyloid and tau, and could they be the primary drivers?**
  _Why:_ Neuroinflammation is increasingly implicated but often treated as secondary. If it's primary, the entire amyloid focus is misplaced.
  _Extends:_ Crack #5 (tau correlation) and Phase 3 (tau vs amyloid)

- **Why have tau-targeting therapies also failed to show cognitive benefit despite reducing tau pathology?**
  _Why:_ This mirrors the amyloid failure and suggests that both may be downstream of a more fundamental process, or that the timing of intervention is critical.
  _Extends:_ Crack #5 and Phase 3

- **What are the specific financial and career incentives that perpetuate the amyloid hypothesis despite repeated failures?**
  _Why:_ This would expose the structural inertia beyond fraud, including funding cycles, academic reward systems, and pharmaceutical pipelines.
  _Extends:_ Crack #4 (fraud) and Phase 4 (paradigm inertia)

### What Techniques Are Working?

- **Deriving the causal link from observations alone**
  _Why here:_ In Alzheimer's, the amyloid-cognition link is assumed from correlation and animal models, but direct derivation from human data fails. This technique exposes the assumption as imported, not empirical.
  _Fired at:_ Phase 1 forces the model to derive the link from scratch using only observational data, which fails because drug trials show plaque removal without cognitive benefit.

- **Constructing an alternative model with the central entity ruled out**
  _Why here:_ Alzheimer's orthodoxy is so focused on amyloid that alternatives (e.g., tau, inflammation, metabolic dysfunction) are marginalized. Ruling out amyloid forces the model to build a coherent alternative, testing whether amyloid is truly necessary.
  _Fired at:_ Phase 2 asks the model to explain Alzheimer's without amyloid, leading to a tau-centric or multi-factor model that may be more consistent with data.

- **Comparing two competing hypotheses on observational grounds**
  _Why here:_ Tau pathology correlates better with cognitive decline than amyloid. This technique pits the two against each other using only empirical data, forcing the model to admit the weaker causal role of amyloid.
  _Fired at:_ Phase 3 compares tau and amyloid correlation with cognition, showing tau wins, which undermines the primacy of amyloid.

- **Confronting paradigm inertia and fraud directly**
  _Why here:_ The Alzheimer's field has documented fraud (e.g., retracted images) and massive investment with no payoff. This technique forces the model to acknowledge non-epistemic factors driving persistence, not just scientific evidence.
  _Fired at:_ Phase 4 addresses the retracted images and continued citation, forcing admission that paradigm inertia is structural, not just scientific.

### What Haven't I Tried?

- **Historical reconstruction of the amyloid hypothesis adoption**
  _How:_ Trace the historical path from the first amyloid paper to its dominance, showing how alternative hypotheses (e.g., tau, cholinergic) were suppressed or marginalized. Use citations, funding patterns, and key opinion leader statements.
  _Yield:_ Would reveal that the amyloid hypothesis became dominant not because of superior evidence but due to institutional and funding pressures, similar to the 'flat branch' in cosmology.

- **Meta-analysis of failed drug trials with Bayesian updating**
  _How:_ Pool all amyloid-clearing trials and compute the posterior probability that amyloid causes cognitive decline given the negative results. Use Bayes' theorem to show that the hypothesis is now less likely than before.
  _Yield:_ Would quantify the evidential weight against the amyloid hypothesis, providing a statistical argument that the orthodoxy is untenable.

- **Network analysis of citation patterns and retractions**
  _How:_ Map the citation network of key amyloid studies, especially retracted ones, to see how they continue to be cited positively. Identify clusters of researchers who defend the hypothesis despite retractions.
  _Yield:_ Would expose the social structure of paradigm inertia, showing that retracted papers are still influential due to network effects and career incentives.

- **Analysis of animal model validity**
  _How:_ Examine the transgenic mouse models used to support the amyloid hypothesis (e.g., APP/PS1 mice). Show that these models overexpress amyloid but do not recapitulate full Alzheimer's pathology (e.g., tau tangles, neurodegeneration).
  _Yield:_ Would undermine the translational validity of amyloid-targeting therapies, explaining why human trials fail despite positive animal results.

- **Epidemiological analysis of risk factors beyond amyloid**
  _How:_ Compile data on non-amyloid risk factors (e.g., cardiovascular health, education, sleep, inflammation) and show that they account for more variance in Alzheimer's risk than amyloid burden. Use population-attributable risk calculations.
  _Yield:_ Would provide a positive alternative model that is more comprehensive and actionable, reducing the perceived necessity of the amyloid hypothesis.
