### What Haven't I Tried?

- **Material Provenance Analysis**
  _How:_ Trace the exact quarry sources of all stone types (limestone, granite, mortar) using isotopic or petrographic analysis. Compare with known ancient quarry sites and check for any materials from non-local or unexpected sources.
  _Yield:_ Could reveal that some stones came from quarries not known to be active in the Old Kingdom, or that materials were transported over distances that challenge the orthodoxy of simple logistics.

- **Acoustic or Seismic Testing**
  _How:_ Use non-invasive acoustic or seismic imaging to map internal cavities, density variations, or construction joints within the pyramid. Compare with expected patterns from ramp-based construction.
  _Yield:_ Could reveal hidden chambers, uniform density suggesting casting, or construction joints inconsistent with block-by-block stacking, supporting alternative construction methods like poured stone.

- **Experimental Archaeology with Replicas**
  _How:_ Build a small-scale replica of a pyramid section using only copper tools, ramps, and manual labor as per orthodoxy. Measure the time, precision, and tool wear achieved. Compare with the actual pyramid's specifications.
  _Yield:_ Would empirically demonstrate the impossibility of achieving the observed precision and placement rate with orthodox methods, providing concrete evidence for the cracks.

- **Statistical Analysis of Block Dimensions**
  _How:_ Collect precise measurements of block dimensions from multiple courses and faces. Perform statistical analysis to check for patterns (e.g., standard deviations, modular ratios) that indicate standardized production or machine cutting.
  _Yield:_ Could reveal that block dimensions follow a tight statistical distribution consistent with machine production, rather than the high variability expected from hand-quarrying and manual placement.

- **Geopolymer or Casting Hypothesis Testing**
  _How:_ Analyze the microstructure of pyramid stones for evidence of re-agglomerated limestone (geopolymer). Use electron microscopy to check for organic binders, air bubbles, or other signs of casting rather than quarrying.
  _Yield:_ If evidence of casting is found, it would completely overturn the orthodoxy of quarried blocks and manual labor, supporting a more technologically advanced construction method.
