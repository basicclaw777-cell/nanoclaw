## Meta-Extraction — The Three Questions

### What Am I Not Asking?

- **What is the specific mechanism by which low-carbohydrate diets improve cardiovascular outcomes in RCTs, and does this mechanism conflict with the lipid hypothesis?**
  _Why:_ It would force a direct comparison between the carbohydrate-insulin model and the diet-heart hypothesis, potentially revealing that the orthodoxy's focus on fat is misplaced.
  _Extends:_ Crack #4 (biomarker contradiction) and Phase 4 (alternative model construction)

- **In the Minnesota Coronary Experiment, what were the actual causes of death in the low-fat group, and were they cardiovascular or non-cardiovascular?**
  _Why:_ It would clarify whether the increased mortality was due to heart disease or other causes, challenging the assumption that cholesterol reduction is beneficial.
  _Extends:_ Crack #1 (cholesterol-mortality paradox)

- **What is the historical evidence that the American Heart Association's dietary recommendations were influenced by industry funding or conflicts of interest?**
  _Why:_ It would expose potential bias in the orthodoxy's formation, similar to the Seven Countries Study selection bias, and undermine trust in the guidelines.
  _Extends:_ Crack #5 (foundational evidence corruption)

- **How do the effects of dietary saturated fat on LDL particle size and number differ from the effects of carbohydrate intake?**
  _Why:_ It would refine the understanding of lipid risk factors, showing that carbohydrate-induced small LDL may be more atherogenic than saturated fat-induced large LDL.
  _Extends:_ Crack #4 (biomarker contradiction)

### What Techniques Are Working?

- **Cascade analysis of contradictory RCTs**
  _Why here:_ Nutrition orthodoxy relies heavily on observational data and weak associations; RCTs provide direct causal evidence that can falsify the hypothesis. The technique leverages the hierarchy of evidence.
  _Fired at:_ Crack #1 and #2 use RCTs (Minnesota Coronary Experiment, Sydney Diet Heart Study) to show that the recommended diet increases mortality.

- **Systematic review meta-analysis**
  _Why here:_ Nutrition claims often depend on selective citation; systematic reviews aggregate all available evidence, revealing lack of support for the orthodoxy.
  _Fired at:_ Crack #3 uses systematic reviews of RCTs showing no significant association between saturated fat and cardiovascular disease.

- **Historical data integrity audit**
  _Why here:_ Foundational studies in nutrition (e.g., Seven Countries Study) are often cited as definitive; auditing their methodology exposes selection bias and data manipulation.
  _Fired at:_ Crack #5 reveals that the Seven Countries Study excluded countries that did not fit the hypothesis.

- **Biomarker pathway contradiction**
  _Why here:_ The orthodoxy predicts that low-fat diets improve biomarkers; showing that they worsen biomarkers (small LDL, triglycerides) directly contradicts the expected mechanism.
  _Fired at:_ Crack #4 shows that replacing saturated fat with carbohydrates increases small LDL and triglycerides.

### What Haven't I Tried?

- **Dose-response analysis of saturated fat intake**
  _How:_ Examine RCTs and observational studies that vary saturated fat intake across a wide range and measure cardiovascular outcomes, looking for a linear or J-shaped relationship.
  _Yield:_ It would likely show no increased risk at moderate intakes and possible harm only at extreme levels, undermining the 'all saturated fat is bad' narrative.

- **Confounding factor decomposition**
  _How:_ Re-analyze observational studies that claim saturated fat is harmful, controlling for confounding variables like smoking, socioeconomic status, and overall diet quality, using techniques like Mendelian randomization.
  _Yield:_ It would likely reveal that the apparent harm from saturated fat disappears when confounders are properly controlled, exposing the weakness of observational evidence.

- **Historical policy impact analysis**
  _How:_ Trace the timeline of dietary guidelines and compare with actual population health outcomes (e.g., obesity, diabetes, heart disease rates) to see if the recommendations correlated with improved or worsened health.
  _Yield:_ It would likely show that the low-fat era coincided with rising obesity and diabetes, suggesting the guidelines may have been counterproductive.

- **Mechanistic plausibility audit of lipid hypothesis**
  _How:_ Examine the biological plausibility of the lipid hypothesis by reviewing evidence on LDL particle size, oxidation, and inflammation, and whether dietary saturated fat actually raises the most atherogenic particles.
  _Yield:_ It would likely reveal that the lipid hypothesis is oversimplified and that saturated fat's effect on LDL is not necessarily harmful, especially when compared to the effects of refined carbohydrates.
