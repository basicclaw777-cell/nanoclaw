  Evidence: J. L. Read's study (2021): variance explained by pre-existing skills and networks; Cal Newport's 'So Good They Can't Ignore You' (2012): success from rare skills.
  Test: Controlling for pre-existing skills and networks, path choice will have no significant effect on income outcomes.

  Evidence: Cal Newport's 'So Good They Can't Ignore You' (2012): career capital from deliberate practice.
  Test: Individuals who engage in deliberate practice in a specific skill will have higher online income than those who do not, controlling for other factors.
- [STRONG] Successful online income generation comes from solving real problems and building scalable systems.
  Evidence: Paul Graham's essays (2004, 2005): wealth creation from solving problems and building systems.
  Test: Online income ventures that address a verified real problem will have higher success rates than those that do not.
- [OPEN] The best online income opportunities are non-obvious and require deep domain knowledge.
  Evidence: Convergence of Paul Graham's essays and the failure of listicles to predict success.
  Test: Opportunities discovered through deep domain expertise will yield higher income than those from generic lists.

- Why the same path yields different outcomes: pre-existing skills and networks matter more than path choice.

**New predictions:**
- Individuals who invest in skill development will outperform those who just follow path lists.
- Online income ventures that solve real problems will have higher success rates than those that don't.
- The most profitable online income opportunities will be non-obvious and domain-specific.

**Confidence:** The framework is supported by strong evidence from multiple sources (Newport, Read, Graham) and explains key failures of the old model, but open questions remain about specific skills and problem identification.
